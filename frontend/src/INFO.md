## Information
Merge, Match, Master! Molecular Booking for Structural Analysis.

In this document you will find background information on certain features.

#### Technical
Most technical information can be found in the <b>Changelog</b> and <b>About</b> tabs.<br/>
The software is in the form of a 'webapp' meaning it runs in what is in essence `Chromium` web browser, but since everything is bundled with the installer, no internet connection, or any installed browser or specific software is required. <br/>
Still, there are a few requirements; you can almost run it on a brick, as long as it has a relatively fresh coat of paint.
```code
Minimal requirements: 
- 1080p resolution without scaling. It works fine on 1080p with 125% scaling or 720p, but is not recommended. 
  The spectra will look grainy with lower resolutions.  
- Windows 10 / macOS 12 / Ubuntu 24 / Fedora 42  (Older and Arch-based Linux distro's: use the flatpak version for now.)
- 2 GB RAM, 1 GB disk space
```

#### Goal
The main purpose of this software is to help students:
>    1)  familiarize themselves with solving organic structures
>    2) familiarize themselves with certain common patterns (e.q. the ethyl group, 1,2,4-trisubstituted benzene derivatives etc.)
>    3)  approach the problems in a systematic way
>    4)  build up their structure from small fragments
>    5)  use matching and annotation to solidify their suspicions and when combined with the other fragments form irrefutable argumentation for the suggested final structure 

The software has several visibility options to increase or decrease the level of difficulty somewhat, even cheats can be used. Note however that the answers to the exercises are encrypted and cannot be decrypted, they are compared against the encrypted user input for validation.<br/>
You can view your progression over time (still in development) per exercise set and level, and how many times you’ve tried the exercise, the time you spent on the exercise and whether or not you used cheats to complete the exercise. Many of these statistics can be reset.
#### Fragments and functional groups
A set of predefined fragments is made available from which you can choose or build most of the functional groups and common side chains. You can also opt to draw everything yourself with the molecular editor. The editor also has a structure library you can use. <br/>
Since the software is based on drawing complete molecules, you cannot simply draw ‘fragments’ with missing atoms. When doing such an exercise on paper it is common to display a fragment a certain way, for example with an arrow instead of a bond, to show where the fragment is connected to another fragment. This practice cannot be duplicated in this software.
The drawn or selected fragments have an hydrogen atom in place where the connection to another fragment would or could be: a methyl group is then methane, and an hydroxyl group is water. You can opt to draw the fragment with another atom as placeholder, but this will take more time to undo later than getting used to the system.<br/>
You can always annotate the fragments to explain certain characteristics of that fragment (next to sp<sup>3</sup> or something). 
#### Merging and matching
One of the key features of this software is its ability to match fragments (as a whole) to the data of the exercise. You can select a peak visually from the spectrum or select it in the data table, then click on the fragment to match it. This can tremendously help with keeping track of the data you’ve already used. It is no coincidence that the software was initially called “Molecular Bookkeeping”. <br/>
Smaller fragments may be merged into larger ones and/or the final structure, which can then be transported to a solution space to be validated. When you merge fragments together it will create a new fragment while retaining the older fragments. Through the options, you can set up how you would like the matching of data to the fragments to be handled when a new merged fragment is created.<br/>
Merging can take place two different ways: just by connecting two existing fragments in the active fragment workspace. Or by selecting one fragment, use that as a solution and then build that solution by merging more fragments to it. The latter option does <em>not</em> create a new fragment every time you combine a fragment to the active solution workspace, but you can opt to send it back to the fragment workspace.
#### Cheats
Like anywhere, cheating is discouraged. Having cheats enabled allows certain information to be visible which can help to solve the structure more easily. Solving structures with cheats are recorded in your progress.

#### 2D-NMR
The use of 2D-NMR is for 95% of the exercises not needed and these 2D spectra are therefore most of the time not included. 
The majority of the exercises are chosen to primarily target a beginner audience, but can be supplemented with more difficult exercises where 2D-NMR use is unavoidable. The difficulty levels 4 and 5 are the ones that in some way or form require the use of 2D-NMR, although other exercises can also be of that level.
2D-NMR is best introduced first to help with structural assignment of each atom. After that, useful small molecule exercises within this software can help distinguish, for example, regioisomers with the use of HMBC or NOESY.

#### Carbon-13 Spectra
At Leiden University it is standard practice to record <sup>13</sup>C-NMR as J-modulated  “Attached Proton Test” (APT) spectra using the (Bruker) `jmod` pulse sequence. The spectral caption will display "APT" for all spectra recorded in such a way. Some samples do not have APT recorded, but use a ‘regular’ broad-band decoupled (bbdec) pulse sequence `zgpg30` or `zg0pg` (with a custom flip angle, the teacher preferred 60 degrees over 30 degrees). <br/>
Some acquired data samples only have ‘bbdec’ data, sometimes deliberately so, but where possible DEPT-135 data (`deptsp135`) has been used to supplement the spectrum in the same picture. In some cases (alkynes for example) APT and DEPT sequences result in a wrong phase for the large deviated C-H coupling constant. In those cases, instead of tweaking the timing in the pulse sequences, it was opted to not include any of the J-modulated data. Instead and when possible, GATED-decoupled data was supplied. Although less commonly used, it provides the same H-coupling information as the J-modulated spectra and is not difficult to interpret for small molecules with non-overlapping signals.

#### Stereochemistry
The molecules to be elucidated can have chiral centers and these may affect how to draw and validate your final answers. In order not to overcomplicate the elucidation process, drawing options for chirality have been removed. Consider the following possibilities:
-  Molecules having 0 chiral centers: just one CAS number, one spectrum, one solution, but the molecule might be pro-chiral and result in diastereotopic nuclei being observable.
-  Molecules having 1 chiral center: the mirror images have identical spectra, drawing without any defined chirality therefore there is one solution, three CAS numbers are usually available (R, S, and racemic) and all three can be used for manual validation, diastereotopic nuclei might be present.
-  Molecules having 2 chiral centers: two enantiomeric pairs of diastereomers with possible meso-isomers, a maximum of four CAS numbers of the pure compounds and several more CAS numbers of the various mixtures. Drawing of a single, ‘flat’, structure will provide the correct answer.

It suffices to say diastereomers are not very well suited to be distinguished using this software, which has never been the purpose anyway. In spite of this, a few molecules having two chiral centers <em>are</em> part of the exercises, and the student is then required to draw the undefined, flat, structure. Usually these are symmetrical structures, also having a <em>meso</em>-isomer, and often the diastereomers have very similar spectra. The connectivity is then the important part of the elucidation. 
The drawn solution must always be the “flat” structure with undefined chirality. The manual CAS input is selected from the three most common isomers (convenient when there is a <em>meso</em>-isomer), and note that no racemic mixture or other mixture/undefined structure CAS number is used.<br/>
Molecules with more than 2 chiral centers are best saved for exercises outside this software. For example: sugar molecules where many 2D-NMR related questions can be asked, the Karplus-relation can be used etc.
#### Infrared spectra
For this project, IR spectra are deemed ‘nice-to-have’ and are by no means the focal point of the structure elucidation course. If they are absent or the data was not as satisfactory when measured, no attempts were made to remeasure or forcefully include or exclude them. If so desired (to save space for example, they are about 230 kB apiece), you can delete all the “*-IR.svg” files from the .zip imports before you start the import process.
#### Bookmarks (in development)
You can add bookmarks to exercises in four different colors. It is up to you what you want to use them for. Maybe you’d like to revisit a certain spectrum, whether they are completed or not, or mark them as interesting, or difficult. Bookmarks can be used to filter the exercise selection. #### Statistics

For each exercise, statistics are stored. This includes when it was first started, halted, and completed; how much time was spent until halt or completion; if any cheats were used; and how often incorrect validation occurred.<br/>
In the statistics panel, the collective data is visualized in several different ways to show insight into study behavior and progression.
